# Content pillars

Rotate so no pillar runs two days in a row on the same platform. Roughly per week:

| Pillar | Share | What it is | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| **build-in-public** | 30% | What shipped this week, what broke, what's next. Screenshots, short demos. | Makes people follow the journey and want to try the app. |
| **feature-spotlight** | 25% | One feature of Slipnote or Setalytix, shown in use, with the problem it solves. | Directly drives installs. |
| **engineering-deep-dive** | 25% | How something was built: architecture, a bug hunt, a trade-off (offline-first sync, widget refresh on Android, free-tier stack). | Builds credibility with developers, recruiters and founders. |
| **lessons** | 15% | Things learned shipping solo: testing the riskiest part first, zero-cost stacks, planning in phases, building with AI. | Broad reach, relatable. |
| **community** | 5% | Asking for beta testers, feedback, feature votes, thanking early users. | Converts audience into users. |

## App split (until Slipnote's launch is near)
- **Setalytix ~60%**: it's live and free, so posts drive sign-ups (`app.setalytix.in`).
- **Slipnote ~35%**: build the waitlist and the story, so people are ready on launch day.
- **Personal ~5%**: who's building this, how he works with AI.

## Platform emphasis
- **LinkedIn:** engineering-deep-dive, product calls (what Harsh decided and why), lessons, build-in-public. 1–2 posts a week end with the open-to-work line from the private `founder.md`.
- **Instagram:** feature-spotlight, build-in-public (visual), community.
- **YouTube Shorts:** feature demos, "how I built X in 30 seconds", talking-head build updates.

## Video mix (Reels + Shorts)
- ~4/week **faceless**: screen recordings from `inbox/` with captions, or slide-based motion videos.
- ~2–3/week **talking-head**: Claude writes the script in the post file (`video.script`), Harsh records it and drops the clip in `inbox/`.
- If a talking-head clip isn't recorded by publish time, the post is skipped and a reminder issue is opened. Nothing half-finished goes out.
