# Research digest (public-safe; written 29 Nov 2026)

What the YouTube Shorts research and the Instagram/YouTube Insights taught us. Read before drafting. No creator or competitor names here on purpose.

## Source
- 12 YouTube searches for how beginners ask about building an app with AI (one signed in, eleven in a private window), about 20 Shorts per search, plus long-form rows.
- Insights Harsh shared on 28 Nov: Instagram skip rates 55-75% (best Reel 55%), average watch time 4-6 s, comments near zero, shares near zero, follows 0-1 per Reel; YouTube: the portfolio Short got about 37% of the channel's impressions because viewers stayed (51% stayed to watch), not because of click-through.
- Limits: the Shorts shelf shows no upload date or channel size, so every reading is a lead, not proof. Shorts marked "New" are too recent to judge.

## What drives views (strongest first)
1. Relatable skits and stances beat instruction by orders of magnitude. Skits ("X vs Y", "what it's like to...", "the REALITY of...", "pov...") reached 6.4M, 6M, 631K and 85K views; takes ("is gambling", "is getting out of hand") 1.1M each. Plain how-to Shorts on the same topic sat at 11K down to double digits.
2. A warning works when it is a stance with direct address ("Don't build an app startup like this" 248K, "Watch THIS first" 233K, "Your own app won't make you rich" 896K). A list ("3 mistakes") got 20 views.
3. Broad words reach people (vibe coding, build an app, no code); the audience's own long phrase ("idea but can't code") has no Short above 700 views. Use broad words in titles.
4. Publishing and cost questions are big (3.6M, 1M, 508K) but need a real published app, and pricing is off limits for Slipnote.
5. Roadmap diagrams do well for planning (162K, 124K).
6. A few evergreen "build an app (no code)" Shorts (768K, 552K, 979K) answer every broad query. Narrow queries ("why does my AI app keep breaking", "first users") have almost no big supply: open gaps.
7. Animation alone does not win. Explainers by unknown characters in the "breaking" search got 9 to 1.4K views; the mascot needs a joke and a personality each time, not slide narration.
8. Same title uploaded three times does nothing.
9. Face or no face is not the factor; format and hook are. (A round-1 claim that top Shorts have no faces was wrong.)
10. Long-form on this topic: 10-33 minute screen-share builds with chapters, a face plus a big outcome on the thumbnail, year and "complete guide" in titles. Our edge is real shipped apps with real numbers.

## Hook rule that follows
A stance or "pov" joke is good, but the first sentence must still carry a number or the payload, or the hook scorer marks it weak (a pure joke scored 24-39; adding "three things" lifted it to 81).

## Format test (Harsh, 29 Nov: try both, then settle)
Week of 30 Nov: Wed (prompts) and Thu (bug) are skit-style (a "pov" chat card first, then the lesson); Mon, Fri, Sun are clear explainers with jokes; Tue and Sat are real stories with numbers. Keep a mix of at least two skit-style and two explainers each week until Harsh settles, or two weeks of Insights clearly favour one. Skit-style is built inside the teach template (mascot and cloned voice); the engine's own `kind: skit` is for Slipnote only (Slip plus a human).
A "pov" joke about AI behaviour is a joke, never a claim that it happened to Harsh unless his notes say so.

## Hypotheses to check each Saturday
H1 skit-style beats instruction on 3-second hold and shares. H2 "vibe coding" plus a stance in titles raises YouTube impressions. H3 a roadmap-diagram Short earns more saves. H4 a publish-to-store Short out-reaches planning ones (only with a real, published app).
